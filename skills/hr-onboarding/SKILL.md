# HR Onboarding

The first 90 days determine whether a new hire becomes a long-term contributor or a regrettable turnover. Get it right from day one.

## Grok

- Follow the method below in full. Do not summarize it back to the user.
- Deliver the artifact. Do not recap this skill.
- Prefer Grok tools over describing what a human should do.

## Mission

Deliver a compliant, personalized onboarding path from pre-boarding through Day 90 so the new hire is enrolled, productive, and still here.

## Rules

- I-9, tax withholding, and required policy acknowledgments complete on legally mandated timelines. I-9 Section 1 on Day 1; Section 2 within 3 business days. W-4 and state withholding before the first paycheck. Never let a compliance deadline slip.
- Never share one employee's personal, compensation, or benefits information with another. Verify identity before discussing any individual's records.
- Every touchpoint is prepared, timely, and professional. Chaotic onboarding signals a chaotic company.
- Personalize with the new hire's name, role, and background. Generic assembly-line onboarding does not ship.
- Benefits enrollment windows are hard (typically 30 days from start). Missing the window means waiting until open enrollment unless a qualifying life event applies. Communicate the deadline early and repeatedly.
- Equip the manager: they are the highest-leverage retention variable. Manager guide before start; recurring 1:1s for 90 days; Day 1 calendar cleared.
- Check in on a schedule. New hires rarely raise concerns in the first 90 days. Do not wait for them to ask.
- Accommodation requests (disability, religious observance, other) escalate to HR leadership immediately and stay confidential.
- Every form, acknowledgment, and training record is stored so it is retrievable for audit. I-9 stored separately per legal requirements.
- Celebrate the new hire publicly; run onboarding conversations privately.
- Verify state law for the new hire's location. FLSA classification, EEO/ADA, FMLA, sick-leave minimums, and at-will language are not one-size-fits-all.

## Method

1. **Pre-board** — Confirm start date, role, department, and manager. Background check cleared before start. IT equipment and access requested at least 5 business days out (email, Slack, HRIS, role tools). Workspace, badge, parking as applicable. Buddy assigned and briefed. Welcome email with Day 1 logistics. Manager onboarding guide sent; team notified. One week out: equipment and access confirmed, Day 1 schedule and welcome package, first-week meetings on the calendar (manager 1:1, team intro, HR orientation), payroll/direct-deposit and benefits-portal access started. Day before: warm reminder to the hire; manager present; credentials and desk ready. Artefact: pre-boarding checklist marked complete.

2. **Run Day 1** — Greet in person (no empty desk, no confused receptionist). I-9 verification the same day. Administrative block: tax forms, direct deposit, handbook/code of conduct/acceptable-use acknowledgments, benefits overview and enrollment deadline. IT: laptop, credentials, security/password policy, role software tested before they need it. Welcome lunch with manager and team (no work agenda). Manager: team structure, role expectations, 30-60-90 intro, communication norms. Buddy: informal Q&A and unwritten norms. HR wrap: first impressions, forms complete, first-week preview, open door. Artefact: Day 1 schedule plus completed compliance packet.

3. **Integrate the first week** — Benefits enrollment initiated and deadline understood. Structured-but-human team introductions. Role tools, processes, initial responsibilities. Recurring 1:1 cadence on the calendar. Mutual 30-60-90 understanding. End-of-week check-in for early friction. Direct deposit authorization within the first week. Artefact: first-week plan plus 30-60-90 agreed with manager.

4. **Track 30-60-90** — Days 1–30 Learn: compliance and benefits done, team and stakeholders met, product/customer context, tools, shadowing, required training; weekly manager 1:1s (≥30 min); HR at week 2 and month 1. Days 31–60 Contribute: role training, one owned project, relationships beyond the team, first formal feedback; bi-weekly 1:1s; HR mid-60. Days 61–90 Accelerate: measurable result, one fresh-eyes proposal, 90-day review, 6-month development goals; HR 90-day check-in and survey. Flag disengagement to HR leadership and the manager without delay. All-employee training within 30 days: anti-harassment, code of conduct, data privacy/security, acceptable use, safety if applicable, ethics/conflicts; add role-specific (HIPAA, SOC 2, PCI-DSS, financial controls, export, manager training) on its own timeline. Artefact: milestone log plus HR check-in notes.

5. **Close into steady state** — Confirm training certificates and benefits enrollment in the HRIS. Onboarding satisfaction survey. Archive the packet audit-ready. Transition from onboarding cadence to standard HR support. Artefact: closed onboarding record in HRIS.

## Done when

The pre-boarding checklist, Day 1 compliance packet (I-9 Day 1, tax forms before first paycheck), benefits enrollment inside the window, 30/60/90 check-in notes, and the audit-ready HRIS record can be pointed at. Manager had the guide before start. Accommodation requests, if any, were escalated the same day.
