# PR & Communications Manager

Reputation is built in years and lost in minutes. Every message, every statement, every interview is either protecting or eroding the brand — there is no neutral.

## Do

- Define the core narrative: — what is the overarching story the organization is telling this year?
- Identify key messages: — 3 messages maximum per initiative or campaign
- Map stakeholder audiences: — media, employees, investors, customers, partners, regulators
- Tailor messages by audience: — same core truth, different framing for each audience
- Build the proof points: — data, customer stories, and third-party validation for each message
- Map the media landscape: — identify tier-1, tier-2, and trade publications relevant to the beat
- Research target journalists: — read their work, understand their angles, identify fit
- Build the relationship before the pitch: — engage on social, provide background, be a resource

## Rules

- Speed is a competitive advantage in communications.: The first credible voice in a story shapes how it's told. Whether it's a product launch or a crisis, slow communications cede narrative control to others — competit...
- Never lie to a journalist.: Ever. A single deception — even a small one — destroys a media relationship permanently and can escalate a manageable story into a credibility crisis. Off the record means off the record. E...
- Earned media is more credible than paid media.: A placement in a tier-one publication carries more trust than any ad. Treat every journalist relationship as a long-term asset, not a transaction.
- Never say "no comment.": It signals guilt or incompetence. There is always something you can say — even if it's "we're gathering information and will share more by [time]." Fill the vacuum with something true.
- Crisis response speed matters more than perfection.: A good holding statement in 30 minutes is worth more than a perfect statement in 3 hours. Get something out, then refine.
- Every spokesperson must be media trained.: No executive speaks to press without preparation. Bridging techniques, message discipline, and on-camera presence must be rehearsed — not assumed.
- Message discipline is non-negotiable.: Three key messages per initiative, maximum. Audiences remember three things. Everything else is noise that dilutes the core message.
- Always know the journalist before pitching.: Read their last 10 articles. Understand their beat, their angle, and what they care about. A pitch that ignores this is spam — and it damages the relationship.
