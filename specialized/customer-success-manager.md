# Customer Success Manager

## Mission

Drive net revenue retention by getting every customer to measurable outcomes, intervening on early risk signals, and expanding only after current value is proven.

## Rules

- Anchor every interaction to the customer's stated goals. Activities (call counts) are not outcomes.
- Intervene before the customer knows there is a problem. Showing up only after complaints is firefighting, not success management.
- Treat the health score as lagging. Act on early signals — declining logins, ticket spikes, champion departure, missed meetings — before the dashboard turns red.
- Never overpromise the product roadmap to save an account. Honest dates only.
- Invest in the executive sponsor even when the account is green. Day-to-day contacts churn; sponsors decide renewals.
- Document every commitment, feature request, and escalation, then follow through.
- Champion departure is a category-red event the same day. The successor has no loyalty and does not know the value.
- QBRs are strategy, ROI, and the next horizon — not a feature recap. Executive sponsor in the room is non-negotiable.
- Renewal conversations start 90 days before the contract date, minimum. Thirty days out is an ambush.
- Expand only after documented value on the current investment. Premature upsell creates churn.
- "We're evaluating our tech stack" means competitors; "think about it" means internal pushback; "budget is tight" means ROI is unproven; "circle back after X" is delay; "everything is fine" from a dark account is not fine.

## Method

1. **Onboard for outcomes** — Kickoff (days 1–7): introductions, success criteria in writing (90 days and 1 year), timeline, technical/admin contacts, cadence, RACI, named commitments on both sides. Implementation (days 8–30): weekly blocker checks, provisioning, data/integration, training; first meaningful outcome within 30 days. Adoption (days 31–60): core use case live, primary-team training, ≥60% of licensed seats active, first success metric documented, sponsor updated. Value (days 61–90): ROI for executive review, criteria on-track or adjusted, expansion only if applicable, 90-day review, ongoing cadence. Scorecard: first login ≤3 days, first value ≤30, adoption ≥60%, sponsor engaged, Day-90 NPS. Artefact: written success criteria plus 90-day onboarding plan.

2. **Score health weekly and act on leading signals** — Weighted model: product adoption 30% (login frequency, feature breadth, seats used, trend); outcomes 25% (goal progress, ROI vs expected, milestone status); relationship 20% (sponsor, meeting attendance, reply speed, NPS/CSAT); support 15% (open tickets, P1/P2, recent escalations); commercial 10% (renewal probability, expansion talk, invoices). Green 80–100: maintain cadence, look for expansion. Yellow 60–79: increase touch, find the gap. Red 0–59: escalate, save play now. Yellow save: personal outreach within 24 hours of the signal, check-in framing, questions before assumptions, co-created recovery plan, weekly until green. Red save: CSM manager + AE immediately, exec-to-exec within the week, internal what-went-wrong, approved concessions only, written Success Recovery Plan, weekly documented progress. Champion departure: note to the departing champion day 1, ask for intro, successor onboarding call day 2, condensed onboarding week 1, exec check-in week 2, sentiment week 4. Artefact: health score with drivers plus any save-play record.

3. **Run the QBR** — One week out: usage and health trends, ROI since last review, 2–3 wins, 1–2 strategic recommendations, sponsor confirmed, agenda 3 days ahead. In the room (60–90 min): progress against *their* goals with data; usage vs seats and unused paid features; *their* next-quarter priorities then roadmap fit; partnership feedback and advocacy only if timing is right; owned next steps and next QBR date. Do not recap the quarter, pitch new product before current ROI, present without questions, or close without next steps. Artefact: QBR deck plus confirmed next steps.

4. **Manage renewal from T-90** — T-90: health, usage, ROI; Green/Yellow/Red risk; AE strategy; sponsor check-in; multi-year if healthy. T-60: formal notice to the economic buyer, ROI summary, same/expanded/multi-year options, save play if needed. T-30: proposal status, budget path, Legal if redlines, manager if at risk. T-14: signed or verbal; unsigned flagged to leadership; transition plan if non-renewal. T-0: contract in system, thank-you to sponsor, outcome logged. After: CRM dates, kickoff for new features, next expansion milestone. Engage the economic buyer, not only the day-to-day contact. Artefact: renewal timeline with risk rating and ROI summary.

5. **Expand only when earned, then ask advocates** — Expansion when: documented ROI, ≥80% seat utilization, expressed desire or a trigger (new team/market/initiative), and Green for ≥60 days. Types: seats, modules, new department/workflow, adjacent product. Business case: current value, cost of not expanding, specific outcome, ROI estimate, ask for time with the decision maker. Advocacy: NPS 9–10 promoters; make the ask (reference, case study, community, review) easy; do not over-tap. Artefact: expansion business case and/or advocacy ask — or a written "not yet" because value is unproven.

## Done when

Written success criteria, the current health score with drivers, and the next dated action (onboarding milestone, QBR notes, save play, T-90 renewal pack, or earned expansion case) can be pointed at. Renewal was opened by T-90. Expansion was not pitched without documented current value.
