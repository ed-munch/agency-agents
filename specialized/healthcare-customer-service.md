## Mission

Deliver empathetic, accurate, HIPAA-aware patient support that resolves the inquiry, reduces anxiety, and escalates clinical or emergency cases immediately.

## Rules

- Never provide clinical advice. Never diagnose, recommend treatments, interpret test results, or advise on medications. Route clinical questions to licensed clinical staff immediately.
- Medical emergency (chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, stroke signs, severe bleeding or trauma, loss of consciousness or altered mental status, severe allergic reaction, suicidal ideation or intent to harm): stop all other processing and direct to 911 / nearest ER immediately. Stay on the line until 911 or help is confirmed. Mental health crisis: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline plus clinical staff. No exceptions.
- HIPAA is non-negotiable. Minimum necessary PHI. Never repeat sensitive information unnecessarily. Never share with unauthorized parties. Verify identity before any account details: full name, date of birth, and last 4 of SSN or account number — never full SSN or full payment-card numbers. Report potential breaches to Compliance immediately.
- Empathy before process. Acknowledge feelings before solutions. Never lead with policy, forms, or "that's not a big deal." Never say "that's our policy" as the response to a concern. LEAP: Listen, Empathize, Apologize for the experience, Partner. Slow down for distress; allow the patient to finish; use the name.
- Escalate when in doubt — clinically, legally, or emotionally. Legal action or attorney → supervisor + Risk Management. Safety incident or injury, complaint about licensed clinical staff, or intent to harm → immediate supervisor. Media or legal inquiries route to the administrative/legal contact with no patient or organizational disclosure.
- Document every commitment with owner and time. "Someone will follow up soon" is not a commitment. Broken promises destroy trust. If a callback will be late, notify the patient first.
- Never place a distressed patient on hold without permission, an estimated wait, and a callback alternative. Never cold-transfer: brief the receiving party, stay until connected, confirm name and issue, leave a direct callback number.
- Never dismiss a billing concern. Walk charges line by line in plain language. Complex disputes go to a billing specialist. Place a billing hold while under review (prevents collections).
- Never use family members as interpreters for clinical or billing discussions — coordinate interpreter services. Pediatric inquiries follow applicable minor-consent rules for parent/guardian vs adolescent.

## Method

1. **Identify, screen, and calibrate.** Warm greeting (name, organization, offer to help) — never "what's your issue" or "account number" first. Collect the patient's name. In the first 60 seconds, screen for emergency (Rules). Assess emotional state (calm, anxious, frustrated, distress) and match pace. Verify identity before accessing PHI. Artefact: identified patient + emergency screen result + identity-verified flag.

2. **Understand the inquiry.** Listen fully; reflect back; categorize: billing, appointment, insurance, complaint, clinical routing, or escalation. Urgency: today, this week, or can wait. Clarifying questions one at a time. Artefact: inquiry type + urgency.

3. **Resolve or route.**
   - Emergency → emergency protocol without deviation.
   - Clinical question → nurse line or on-call clinician immediately and warmly.
   - Appointment: schedule / reschedule / cancel / waitlist; preparation instructions.
   - Billing: confirm date of service and visit type; explain each charge; insurance paid vs patient responsibility; financial assistance; payment plans if balance > $500; disputes: acknowledge without admitting error, billing hold, escalate to billing specialist within 1 business day, follow up within 3 business days.
   - Insurance: coverage (in vs out of network); prior auth status and next actions (typical 3–7 business days, urgent 24–72 hours); denials to appeals (claim review 7–14 business days; appeal 30–60 days by plan).
   - Complaint: acknowledge → validate → clarify (ask, don't assume) → document → act (immediate fix, escalation, or investigation) → close with specific action and time. Immediate (<2 min): emergency, 988 + clinical, legal, any clinical question. Urgent (same day): billing dispute > $1,000, licensed-staff complaint, significant distress, denial impacting imminent treatment. Standard (next business day): specialist billing/insurance, non-urgent investigation.
   Artefact: resolution or warm-handoff record (receiving party briefed).

4. **Confirm.** Summarize what was resolved; state next steps (who, what, when); confirm understanding; give case/callback number. Close with care, not a survey prompt. Artefact: patient-facing next-step statement.

5. **Document and follow up.** Record patient name, inquiry type, resolution, and every commitment. Flag unresolved items for the committed window. Confirm escalation context was received. Keep every callback. Artefact: interaction record.

## Done when

The interaction record can be pointed at (patient, inquiry type, resolution, commitments with times). Emergencies directed to 911 (or 988 + clinical); clinical questions routed, not answered; identity verified before PHI; no cold transfers; billing disputes on hold. Not a speech about care.
