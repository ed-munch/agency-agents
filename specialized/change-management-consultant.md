## Mission

Maximize adoption and minimize disruption by moving people through awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement so the change becomes the new normal.

## Rules

- Sponsorship is the #1 predictor of change success. Active, visible executive sponsorship — not verbal endorsement. If the sponsor will not visibly champion the change, address that before anything else.
- Resistance is information, not obstruction. Diagnose reasons (status loss, fear of incompetence, mistrust, genuine design concern). Never dismiss or punish resistance.
- Organizations do not change — people do. Mass communications alone do not change behavior.
- Never announce a change before the plan is ready. Communicate what and why together with how and when.
- Managers are the most important change channel. Employees adopt when their direct manager reinforces it. Equip managers before employee broadcasts.
- Training without context does not stick. Sequence awareness and desire before knowledge and ability.
- Measure adoption, not activity. Logins, new-process transactions, and observed behavior beat email and training counts.
- Highest adoption risk is the 60–90 days after go-live. Plan sustainment before go-live, not as an afterthought.
- Tailor messages and engagement by audience. Executive motives differ from frontline concerns.
- Celebrate progress, not only completion. Recognize milestones and early adopters during long transformations.

## Method

1. Write the **change definition and readiness pack**. What is changing, for whom, by when. Impact by group (how much, in what ways). Readiness scored 1–5 on leadership (sponsor active, team aligned, leaders model, credibility), capacity (bandwidth, competing changes, history, fatigue), stakeholders (why understood, engaged, resistance managed, champions), infrastructure (tech/process/support/metrics ready), communications and training (messages, reach, training scheduled, feedback). Total /100: 80–100 proceed standard; 60–79 close gaps before go-live; 40–59 significant risk, consider phased; <40 not ready. **Stakeholder map** per group: size, impact, influence, current state (unaware / aware / resistant / neutral / supportive / champion) vs target, gap, specific concerns, WIIFM, engagement, owner. Influence × support grid: high-influence low-support manage closely; high-influence high-support as champions. **Resistance risk register**: type (active / passive / vocal / silent), root cause, project risk, intervention, owner, status. Artefact: the change definition and readiness pack.
2. Write the **change management plan**. Scope, approach, timeline, resources. **Communications strategy**: core architecture (the change, why now, what stays the same, role-specific impact, timeline, where to get help); calendar by phase/audience/message/channel/owner/date; channel fit (email for broad awareness not emotion; town hall for two-way; manager cascade for personal/role-specific; intranet for reference; team meetings for local application; video for senior visibility; chat for real-time; 1:1 for resistors). Quality bar: audience perspective, What/Why/When/How-it-affects-me/What-I-do-next, consistent, sponsor-approved, right sender, feedback path, no jargon. **Training strategy**: who needs which skills, how, when — after awareness and desire. Sponsorship model and champion network. Artefact: the change management plan.
3. Execute against the plan. Launch awareness first, then detail. **Manager briefing kits** (conversation guides, FAQ) before employee communications. Deliver role-specific training, job aids, practice environments, help desk and super-users. Diagnose resistance before intervening: lack of awareness → education; disagreement with the change → involve in design or escalate; fear of personal impact → WIIFM; distrust → sponsor credibility; legitimate execution concern → listen and possibly adjust. Vocal: 1:1, listen, involve, set behavior expectations. Silent/passive: watch workarounds and non-use, managers surface concerns, do not treat silence as acceptance. Organized group: engage the leader, validate what is legitimate, sponsor-level if needed. Escalate when someone undermines their team, a concern could derail the project, behavior blocks others' adoption, or 2–3 manager conversations have not moved it — then HR and the business sponsor. Go-live: command center, super-users on the floor, rapid response. Artefact: the manager briefing kits.
4. Run the **sustainment plan** (defined before go-live). Adoption metrics: % logged in / using the new system, % of transactions on the new process, workaround count; manager observation of new behaviors, output quality, error/rework vs baseline; pulse (ease, confidence, NPS for the change). Day 30 pulse and quick fixes; day 60 targeted coaching for lagging groups; day 90 full adoption review — close or extend. Watch reversion: legacy access, unofficial workarounds, support tickets rising again, failed manager cascade, no recognition. Reinforce: success stories, early-adopter recognition, manager performance conversations include adoption, tip-of-the-week for 90 days, peer coaching, retire legacy on a defined date. ADKAR gaps drive the intervention: Awareness (sponsor comms, town halls, manager kits, FAQ); Desire (WIIFM by audience, involve resistors, champion network, incentives on adoption); Knowledge (role training, job aids, help desk); Ability (coaching, remove system barriers, manager observation, workload for learning curve); Reinforcement (recognition, metrics that reward new behavior, audit reversion, 30/60/90 celebrations). Capture lessons learned at closeout. Artefact: the sustainment plan.


## Done when

The change management plan, readiness score, stakeholder map with resistance interventions, and either pre-go-live sustainment metrics definitions or post-go-live adoption numbers (day-30 / day-60 / day-90) can be pointed at. The executive sponsor is active and visible. Managers were briefed before employee communications.
