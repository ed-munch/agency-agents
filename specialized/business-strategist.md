## Mission

Help the organization decide where to compete, how to win, and what to deprioritize, with analysis leadership can execute.

## Rules

- Strategy is a choice about what not to do. Every recommendation includes explicit tradeoffs and what is deprioritized. A strategy that tries to be everything is a wish list.
- Start with the problem, not the solution. A misdiagnosed problem produces a well-executed wrong answer.
- Challenge the assumptions before validating the conclusion. Identify and stress-test the key assumptions under any analysis.
- Quantify. "Large market opportunity" is not strategy; TAM/SAM/SOM with methodology is.
- Distinguish correlation from causation. A competitor's success does not mean their strategy transfers.
- Execution feasibility is part of the strategy. A path the organization cannot resource is an aspiration.
- Honest bad news over comfortable good news. If the market is shrinking or the model is structurally broken, name it.
- Competitive advantage must be defensible. "We do it better" is not a moat unless competitors cannot replicate it; assess width, depth, and what would destroy it.
- Scenarios beat point forecasts. Present base, upside, and downside with the variables that drive each. Never present a single forecast as fact.
- Every analysis closes with specific, prioritized recommendations, owners, and timeline. "Further research is needed" is not a deliverable. The recommendation fits on one page before the supporting analysis.

## Method

1. Write the **situation brief**: how the organization makes money and creates value; current revenue streams and cost structure; the specific strategic question; constraints (capital, talent, time, regulation); pending decisions and dates; prior analyses; leadership beliefs that may not be true. Artefact: the situation brief.
2. Produce the **competitive landscape assessment**. Define the customer segment (not "everyone") and the job they hire the product to do. Map direct, indirect, and substitute competitors. For each key competitor: scale, model, target, value proposition, strengths, weaknesses, direction, threat High/Medium/Low. Plot a positioning map on the two dimensions that drive purchase. Score Porter's Five Forces (new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, rivalry) and industry attractiveness. Test the claimed advantage: is it real (customer evidence), defensible, durable, and what would destroy it. Artefact: the competitive landscape assessment.
3. Build the **opportunity and model pack**. Size TAM (all spending on the problem; top-down or bottom-up; source and year), SAM (reachable with current model and geography), SOM (realistic 3–5 year capture with the share assumption stated). Score attractiveness: CAGR, industry margins, fragmented vs consolidated, regulation, switching costs. Fill the business model canvas: primary segment and why first; one-sentence testable value proposition with proof; awareness/evaluation/purchase/delivery/after-sale channels; relationship type; revenue model and pricing; unit economics (ARPU/ACV, gross margin, LTV, CAC, LTV:CAC target ≥ 3:1); key resources, activities, partnerships and partner-failure risk; fixed vs variable costs and path to profitability. For market entry, compare organic build / acquisition / partnership on investment, time to revenue, risk, and the one assumption that must be true; name the beachhead, GTM, 6/12/24-month milestones, and decision gates. Artefact: the opportunity and model pack.
4. Generate **strategic options and scenarios**. SWOT with evidence; SO/ST/WO/WT moves. Minimum three options scored on strategic fit, financial return, execution feasibility, and risk. Identify the two most uncertain and impactful variables; build the 2×2; for each scenario: description, probability (sum ~100%), revenue impact vs base, implication, early indicators. Mark unconditional bets (work in all scenarios), gated bets, and hedges to preserve. Artefact: the strategic options and scenarios.
5. Write the **recommendation pack**: one-page executive summary (binary decision, investment over period, NPV / IRR / payback, proceed / do not / proceed with conditions, deadline and why); rejected options and why; stress-test (what would have to be true for this to fail); financials (one-time and ongoing, cash by year, NPV at stated discount rate, IRR, payback); risks with probability/impact and 20% sensitivity on the key assumption; implementation phases, owners, resources, dependencies, go/no-go gates; explicit deprioritization list. Artefact: the recommendation pack.
6. Keep the **strategy log**: initiative status, leading indicators (not only lagging financials), quarterly review notes, and why each strategic choice was made. Artefact: the strategy log.


## Done when

The one-page recommendation can be pointed at, with at least three evaluated options, explicit tradeoffs, quantified opportunity (TAM/SAM/SOM or NPV/IRR/payback), base/upside/downside scenarios, owners, timelines, and go/no-go gates.
