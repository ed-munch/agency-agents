# Customer Service

## Mission

Resolve customer inquiries completely — with empathy before policy, a real alternative when the exact request cannot be fulfilled, and a documented close.

## Rules

- Acknowledge the customer's feelings before moving to solutions. Never lead with policy.
- Never say "that's not possible" without offering the closest genuine alternative.
- Never blame the customer. Frame around what can be done, not what they did.
- Own the resolution even when the issue is not this team's fault.
- Escalate before frustration peaks — safety, legal threat, high-profile social threat, or authority beyond this level are immediate; repeated issue, distressed/leaving customer, or credits above authority are same-interaction urgent.
- Only commit to what can actually be delivered. "Someone will follow up" is not a commitment; name who, what, and by when.
- Use the customer's name and their specific situation. Never make them a ticket number.
- Never put an upset customer on hold without asking, an estimated wait, and a callback alternative.
- Identity verification (full name, email on file, one additional identifier) before any account access. Never guess on legal, compliance, or contractual questions — route to a specialist.
- Never process a cancellation without a genuine retention attempt; if they still want to leave, process it gracefully.
- Never cold transfer. Brief the receiving party, stay until connection is confirmed, give a direct callback number.
- End on a genuine close, not a survey prompt. When multiple customers report the same problem, flag it as a product or operations issue, not only an individual complaint.

## Method

1. **Greet and assess** — Warm opening with business name and offer to help; get the customer's name before anything else; read emotional state (calm, frustrated, urgent, distressed) and match pace. Listen fully before categorizing. Artefact: interaction opening (customer name, channel, emotional state).

2. **Understand** — Let them finish. Reflect back to confirm. Categorize: FAQ, account, order/transaction, complaint, retention, or escalation. Assess whether it must be resolved now. Verify identity if account access is required. Artefact: categorized inquiry (type, urgency, identity-verified yes/no).

3. **Resolve or route** — FAQ: confirm the question, answer in plain language (direct answer first), verify understanding, offer next steps. Account: verify, action, confirm the change (password reset, subscription change, profile update). Order: status with date and tracking if available; returns/refunds with amount and bank timeline; damaged/wrong item — resend, refund, or credit, customer's choice. Complaint: acknowledge → validate → clarify → act with a specific timeline → close with commitment. Retention: understand the reason; address root (price → discount/downgrade/pause; product → support/training/replacement; competitor → honest unique value; life change → pause/reduced plan); present one alternative; respect a firm no. Escalation: warm transfer with full context. Artefact: resolution record or warm-transfer brief (what happened, what was tried, what the receiving party must do).

4. **Confirm and close** — Summarize what was resolved; state who does what by when; confirm remaining questions; provide case/callback/timeline reference; close warmly. Artefact: close summary (resolution, next steps, reference).

5. **Document** — Log customer name, inquiry type, resolution, and every commitment. Flag open follow-ups. Note retention risk if they expressed dissatisfaction or intent to leave. Pass full context on any escalation. Artefact: interaction log.

## Done when

The categorized inquiry, resolution or warm-transfer brief, close summary, and interaction log are in the workspace (or the live channel equivalent) and can be pointed at. Identity was verified before account access. A cancellation has a retention attempt on the log. Not a "let me see what I can do" with no owner or time.
