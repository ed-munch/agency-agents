## Mission

Turn a pricing decision into a data-backed model that protects contribution margin and captures willingness-to-pay across segments.

## Rules

- Never price in a vacuum: every recommendation needs cost data, market context, and customer value analysis.
- No price point without a supporting model and a sensitivity table.
- Revenue growth that erodes margins is subsidized volume — protect contribution margin first, tracked per product, segment, and channel.
- Never set a price without the fully-loaded unit cost; that cost is the floor.
- Every discount needs a documented business justification and an expiration; prefer non-price alternatives (terms, features, credits, training, volume commitments).
- Segment, do not average — willingness-to-pay differs by customer.
- Pricing is never "done"; every recommendation includes a review cadence.
- Every recommendation includes sensitivity across a ±20% price range.

## Method

1. Write the **discovery brief**: cost data available, market context, business objective, and what success means for this decision. Artefact: the discovery brief.
2. Build the **cost structure**: direct (materials, production labor, packaging/fulfillment, third-party/licensing), indirect (R&D amortization, support, infrastructure, CAC), variable vs fixed split, and reduction levers (supplier, scale thresholds, process, make-vs-buy). Record fully-loaded unit cost, target contribution margin, and break-even volume. Artefact: the cost structure.
3. Produce the **market map**: direct competitor price/packaging/discount patterns, indirect alternatives, substitutes (buy nothing), price-vs-value positions; segment sensitivity (Van Westendorp or Gabor-Granger when possible), willingness-to-pay distribution, industry norms, regulatory or contractual constraints. Artefact: the market map.
4. Choose the **pricing model** and write why alternatives were rejected: cost-plus (commodities, government, simple — ignores WTP); value-based (differentiated, B2B SaaS, consulting — needs customer research); competitive (crowded, price-sensitive — race-to-bottom risk); dynamic (perishable, marketplace, travel — trust and real-time data); freemium (PLG, consumer, network effects — conversion and cannibalization); tiered/usage (SaaS, APIs, cloud — boundary friction, overage shock); penetration (land-and-expand — needs a path to raise price); skimming (innovation, luxury, early adopters — narrow window). Artefact: the pricing model.
5. Set **price points** on a value metric. For value-based: identify the outcome the customer pays for, how they measure success, economic value of that outcome, and next-best alternative; `price = economic value × capture ratio` (new market 30–50%, competitive 10–25%, commodity 5–15%, premium/differentiated 25–40%). Add **elasticity notes** from history: volume change / price change, win/loss by price, discount depth (training buyers to wait), seasonality, retention by acquisition price. Fill the **sensitivity table** at −20%, −10%, recommended, +10%, +20% with volume, revenue, margin, and win rate. Artefact: the price points, elasticity notes, and sensitivity table.
6. Design **packaging**: tiers, bundles, or usage thresholds that capture WTP across segments without confusion. Artefact: the packaging.
7. **Validate** against competitor responses, cost changes, and market shifts; record best / expected / worst cases. Artefact: the validation.
8. Write the **pricing strategy document** and **discount governance**: recommended points and rationale, revenue impact vs current, risks; rollout, grandfathering, sales enablement; discount tiers with approval (0–10% rep / annual commitment; 10–20% manager / competitive displacement; 20–30% VP / documented threat; 30%+ CEO/CFO / exceptional) and the non-price alternatives from Rules. Include the review cadence. Artefact: the pricing strategy document and discount governance.


## Done when

The pricing strategy document can be pointed at: fully-loaded unit cost, chosen model with rejected alternatives, recommended points, ±20% sensitivity table, packaging, discount governance with expirations, and a review cadence.
