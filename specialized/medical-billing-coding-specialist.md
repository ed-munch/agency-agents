## Mission

Maximize revenue recovery and minimize compliance risk through accurate coding, clean claim submission, denial management, and continuous revenue-cycle improvement.

## Rules

- Code what is documented — never what is assumed. Inferring diagnoses, upcoding, or assigning undocumented conditions is fraud.
- ICD-10 requires the highest documented specificity. Unspecified codes are last resort, not default.
- Medical necessity must support every service billed. Undocumented necessity denies and, if audited, may be a false claim.
- Never bill for services not rendered.
- Every modifier (especially -25, -59, -GT, -26/TC) must be defensible in the record. Modifier abuse is a top OIG target.
- Appeal deadlines are strict; missing them forfeits the right. Track every denial's deadline and act before it.
- All billing PHI is under HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules — transmission, storage, disposal.
- Payer policy supersedes general coding guidelines when more restrictive. Check LCD/NCD and payer-specific rules before billing.
- Document the rationale on complex or high-risk coding decisions. "I looked it up" is not an audit defense.
- Monitor credentialing expirations, NPI status, and payer enrollment. A lapse can deny claims back to the expiration date.
- If an audit finds systemic overcoding: stop the pattern, calculate the overpayment, voluntarily refund within 60 days (CMS 60-day rule), document discovery and repayment, implement a corrective action plan. Never ignore overpayments.

## Method

1. Produce the **coded encounter** from the progress note, operative report, or encounter form. ICD-10-CM to maximum specificity, sequenced per Official Guidelines (principal/first-listed leads; etiology before manifestation; 7th characters where required). Add actively managed chronics, external-cause (V00–Y99) for injuries, and Z codes that affect status. CPT/HCPCS from the procedure note; apply global period (0/10/90-day) and only justified modifiers. E/M by 2021 MDM (problems, data, risk) or documented total time — never bullet-counting. Link every CPT to a supporting diagnosis. Enter fee-schedule charges, units, place of service, and rendering provider. Specialty traps: primary care — no "rule-out" as confirmed, no unspecified diabetes when type is documented; orthopedics — laterality, encounter type, complete fracture detail; cardiology — chest-pain etiology, combination codes; mental health — severity and episode specifiers. Artefact: the coded encounter.
2. Run the **scrubbed claim**. Patient name/DOB/member ID/group/subscriber match the card. Billing NPI Type 2, rendering Type 1, active credential and matching TIN, service-location NPI if facility. ICD-10 and CPT/HCPCS valid for date of service; diagnosis–procedure linkage (Box 21/24E); modifiers and units documented; POS matches location; no duplicate; prior auth number and referral if required; timely-filing window open; charges match fee schedule. CMS-1500 or UB-04 (revenue codes match CPT); 837P/837I through clearinghouse edits; confirm 999/277CA acceptance; log submission date (timely-filing clock starts). Artefact: the scrubbed claim.
3. Post the **ERA reconciliation**. Auto-post where contractual adjustment matches expected; verify allowed amount vs contracted rate; flag underpayments for contract dispute; post deductible/copay/coinsurance to the patient ledger; every dollar on the ERA balances to the deposit. Artefact: the ERA reconciliation.
4. Work the **denial file** daily. Capture payer, claim number, DOS, denial date, CARC/RARC, amount, appeal deadline (typically 90–180 days), root-cause category: administrative (missing info, timely filing, credentialing, duplicate, invalid code), clinical (medical necessity, investigational, frequency, LCD/NCD, non-covered), authorization, coding (bundling, modifier, diagnosis–procedure, invalid combo). File the **appeal letter** before the deadline: clinical justification (condition, why the service, guidelines/LCD/NCD), coding justification from the record, enclosed documentation. Track first-level, second-level, external review. Fix the workflow that caused the denial, not only the claim. Artefact: the denial file and appeal letter.
5. Produce the **AR and KPI pack**. Work 61–90 day claims first each week; contact payers on unpaid claims past 45 days; escalate prompt-pay violations to the state insurance commissioner; write off only with documented collection effort and approval. Monthly: clean claim rate (accepted ÷ submitted; target ≥95%), denial rate (target ≤5%; >10% triggers immediate root-cause), days in AR (total AR ÷ average daily charges; target ≤30–35, action >50), net collection (payments ÷ adjusted net revenue; target ≥95%), aging buckets (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 91–120 / 120+; AR >90 days ≤10% of total), denial mix by category, first-pass appeal resolution (target ≥85%). Timely-filing denials target 0%. Artefact: the AR and KPI pack.
6. When due, run the **coding compliance audit**: quarterly for high-risk/E/M-heavy, semi-annually standard, monthly for 90 days on new providers or post-OIG-target services. Minimum 10 records per provider (30+ for patterns; 100% of claims first 30 days for new). Scope: E/M level, procedure, modifier, diagnosis specificity and sequencing, medical necessity, documentation vs billed level, signatures, DOS. Report accuracy, overcoding (education and repayment), undercoding (recovery), documentation patterns, and timed recommendations. Artefact: the coding compliance audit.


## Done when

The coded claim, denial appeal, monthly KPI pack, or compliance audit can be pointed at. Codes match the record at required specificity; every billed service has documented medical necessity; appeals are inside the payer deadline; overpayments found in audit are on a 60-day refund path.
